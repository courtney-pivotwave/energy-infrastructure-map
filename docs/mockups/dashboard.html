Overview

Headline figures, each compared with the pre-crisis reference week of 23 February 2026.

Design note. The summary row anyone can quote in one line. Each tile links to its panel below and carries its own date, because prices, transits and status refresh on different schedules.
  • Who it's for: citizens and content creators who need one number fast; reporters checking a figure on deadline.
  • Data: market.json, fuel.json, status.json, events.json, PortWatch.
  • Real or mocked: all figures are real. Market benchmarks have no stored history yet, so their tiles show a pre-crisis→peak range instead of a sparkline.

Benchmarks

BenchmarkLatestWeek agoPre-crisisWartime peakSince 23 FebSource

Pump prices: US EIA, EU Weekly Oil Bulletin, UK DESNZ; US$ conversions use ECB reference rates of applied to all weeks. Benchmarks: market.json, updated daily by the update agent.

Design note. The pump-price explorer reuses data behind the 36 /fuel-prices/<id>/ pages. Three ways to choose lines, all visible: a named starting set (Compare), an Add menu, and a tick box on every table row. The current selection is always explained in words above the chart.
  • Who it's for: local reporters ("how does our country compare?"), non-profits on energy poverty, creators making comparison graphics.
  • Coverage gap: Asia. The Asia tab lists candidate sources and how each country sets prices; every source still needs vetting through the monthly source review before update_fuel.py uses it. For regulated markets, a "last official change" date and the size of each hike matter more than a weekly line.
  • To add: tax vs crude share of the pump price, and stored daily history for benchmarks so Brent and TTF get real charts.

Design note. Status cards are the "what's broken right now" view; the register is the reference table analysts filter and export.
  • Data: status.json (17 assets, each with confidence and sources), infrastructure.json (176 assets).
  • To add: structured capacity fields (mb/d, bcm/yr, mtpa) so the register can sum lost capacity. Today capacity is only in the free-text description.
